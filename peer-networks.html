How to read this table

  • Neighbor network means the first network outside the provider on a traceroute path. It can be a peer, a transit provider or a customer; traceroute cannot tell which relationship it is.
  • IPv4 / IPv6 regions count the provider regions from which the network was seen as a neighbor in that address family. Region chips outlined in blue are regions where at least one hand-off crossed a listed IXP port.
  • Regions are where the traceroutes started, not where the interconnection is. A provider's backbone carries traffic from every region to the hand-off nearest the destination, so a network seen from all regions is often one interconnection reached from everywhere. For example, from both Frankfurt and Hong Kong, AWS reaches MIT (AS3) at the same address, 18.4.212.1.
  • Also a neighbor of lists the other providers whose traceroutes in the same campaign saw this network as a neighbor, in either address family and from any region. It says the network connects to both providers, not that it connects to them in the same place.
  • Observations counts how many times a traceroute crossed from the provider into this network, summed over all regions. A neighbor seen once deserves the least trust: a router that does not answer can make the next network look adjacent. For example, Azure's Dubai region appears to hand off directly to Columbia University (AS14) in a single trace; four other traces to the same Columbia router show NYSERNet in between. Raise Seen at least to hide such cases.
  • Interfaces counts distinct neighbor-side addresses observed across all regions. A low count next to many regions is the signature of that single, shared hand-off.
  • An IXP port resolves to the member network behind it, before any prefix match. IXP membership comes from EuroIX, PCH and PeeringDB, captured on 13 October 2026.
